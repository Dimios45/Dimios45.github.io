Hi, I'm Tirth Gada.

I'm a third-year electrical engineering student at VJTI Mumbai with a minor in robotics. I've been working in robot learning for over a year, and what interests me is deploying policies on real hardware to find out which ones hold up, what they lack, and what makes them generalise.

I've chased that through sim-to-real RL for manipulation, tactile sensors I built for visuotactile imitation learning, and Open Duck Mini, an open-source bipedal platform running RL policies onboard.

I'm currently working on democratising the entire robot learning stack to develop robot foundation models.

the journey so far

One continuous thread: train it, deploy it, see what breaks.

01

I started with what felt like the toughest challenge out there: sim2real.

Taking RL policies trained entirely in simulation and deploying them on real hardware. Building on Stone Tao's work, I deployed sim2real cube picking with PPO on the SO-100 arm, then tested FPO and FPO++ against it on the same task.

02

Then locomotion, so sim2real covered both ends.

I built Open Duck Mini, an open-source bipedal platform, and ran RL walking policies onboard. Manipulation and locomotion, both trained in sim and both walking out into the real world. A humanoid is manifesting soon.

03

Then I wanted to push the limits of extracting value from robot data.

I tried out vision-only ACT, and worked on visuotactile policy learning using FlexiTac, piezoresistive tactile sensors. I also deployed a B-spline diffusion policy on the YAM arms using data collected with our VR teleop kit.

04

Now: hill-climbing foundation models with our own stack.

VR teleoperation on bimanual YAM arms for human-in-the-loop corrections, and fine-tuning VLAs on what comes back. The question is still the same one I started with: what holds up on real hardware, and what makes it generalise.

two things that steered me here

A robotics club, and a lab that threw me in the deep end.

SRA, VJTI's robotics club, with my friends.

We taught Wall-E, a line-following and self-balancing robot, and MARIO, a ROS 2-based manipulator, to 200+ first-years, trying to light the same spark that was lit in me when I sat in those workshops in my own first year.

We also reached the finals of SIH 2025, India's largest hackathon, where we tried to find the best possible way to test gold purity without invasive methods.

HiRO Lab, IISc: hammered into research I had no prior knowledge of.

Learning from a single generated video demo, for which I had to optimise object and hand tracking. And pretraining multi-embodiment grasping agents like Kinematics Flow and GeoMatch.
